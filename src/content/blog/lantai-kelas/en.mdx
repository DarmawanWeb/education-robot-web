The line-following mission almost always works in the simulator, then fails on the first try with a real robot. The cause is rarely the program. It is usually the floor.

## How a line sensor sees

The line sensors on SD v1 and SMP v1 are infrared sensors. They shine light at the floor and measure how much comes back. White surfaces reflect a lot, black tape reflects little. The program only gets a number and decides whether that number means tape or floor.

In the simulator, white floor reads about 80 and black tape about 1000. We put the threshold in the middle, at 500. In a classroom the numbers are rarely that tidy.

## The three usual troublemakers

**Shiny tiles.** Freshly mopped ceramic reflects light like a mirror. In some spots the reflection hits the sensor from the wrong angle and the reading jumps.

**Grout lines.** Dark grout about as wide as the tape can pass for a track. A robot that turns for no reason is often following the grout.

**Sunlight.** Light from the window carries infrared too. A sensor in direct sun can read white floor as something else entirely.

## A five-minute calibration before the mission

This step is part of the calibration meeting in the line module. Repeat it whenever the robots move to another room.

1. Put the robot on white floor and write down the sensor reading.
2. Slide the robot until the sensor sits right over the tape, and write it down again.
3. Take the number halfway between them as the threshold.
4. Test at three spots on the track: by the window, in the middle of the room, and in the darkest corner.

If the gap between the white and black readings is less than a third of the range, change the track. No program can reliably tell apart two colours that read almost the same.

<Note>
  Ask one student to keep the numbers. That list helps again in the debugging meeting, when a
  robot keeps failing at the same spot.
</Note>

## The tape and surface we suggest

Use matte black tape, 18 to 20 mm wide. Shiny electrical tape sometimes reads brighter than it should. For the surface, white card or a matte vinyl banner is far kinder to the sensor than ceramic tiles.

If the room is difficult, one printed track banner can save a lot of lessons. The Arena Pack we are preparing has mats with exactly the same tracks as the simulator arenas, so the threshold numbers can be compared directly.

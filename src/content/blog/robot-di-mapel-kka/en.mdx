With Ministerial Regulation 13 of 2025, Coding and Artificial Intelligence (Koding dan Kecerdasan Artifisial, KKA) became an elective subject in Indonesian schools. It is rolling out in stages from the 2025/2026 school year, starting with grades 5, 7, and 10, and will eventually cover grades 5 to 12.

The natural next question: does a robot fit this subject, and where?

## The elements

For primary and junior high, KKA has four elements: computational thinking, digital literacy, AI literacy and ethics, and using and developing artificial intelligence. Senior high adds two more: algorithms and programming, and data analysis.

Robots are strongest at computational thinking. Ordering steps, repeating patterns, and deciding with if-then rules feel real when the result is a robot moving on the classroom floor. On SD v1, that is what modules 2 to 6 are about.

Digital literacy fits naturally too. Students see for themselves that sensors are data inputs, a controller processes them, and the motors, screen, and buzzer are outputs. On SMP v1, the Wi-Fi telemetry module brings questions closer to daily life: which data may be sent, where to, and what happens when the network drops.

## What a simple robot cannot cover

SD v1 and SMP v1 do not teach artificial intelligence. Both robots run rules that students write. They do not learn from data.

What they can do is start a conversation. In the SMP sound module, students compare the clap-controlled robot they built with the voice assistant on a phone. Both listen, but only one recognises words. Small discussions like this touch the AI literacy and ethics element.

Actually using and developing AI, such as training an object detector, belongs to SMA v1. That kit is still in development, so we are not promising a date yet.

## How schools can run it

According to BBPMP Central Java, KKA can run as an elective, be woven into other subjects, or happen as an after-school activity, using internet-based, plugged, or unplugged approaches. Robots belong to the plugged route. For grades 1 to 4, which KKA does not reach yet, the same primary modules can run as an after-school club.

The mapping for each level, with the modules that cover each element, is in the KKA section of every syllabus on the [curriculum](/en/curriculum) page.
